    Daily research journal — 2026-07-28¶

    Objective¶

    Complete the manual interpretation of the six-video FTS baseline, convert the verdicts into reproducible evidence, and align the next technical work with the supervisor’s scope guidance.

    Supervisor meeting¶

    A meeting took place from 10:00 to 10:45. The supervisor requested continuous, scientific documentation of the work, including failed attempts, corrections, and reasons for method choices. The supervisor also advised completing one tracking target before expanding the system and treating detailed statistics as secondary for now. The FTS was selected as the first target.

    Detailed notes are stored in docs/meeting_notes/2026-07-28_supervisor.md.

    Work completed¶

    • Completed the structured manual review of all baseline candidates.
    • Verified that 130 of 130 unique candidates have a final decision.
    • Generated machine-readable and human-readable review summaries.
    • Added a regression test for latest-decision semantics.
    • Prevented unchanged review decisions from being saved repeatedly.
    • Updated the FTS roadmap: candidate-level baseline complete; ground-truth dataset active.
    • Established the scientific journal, decision register, lesson register, experiment register, and reconstructed project history.
    • Defined the one-class FTS annotation protocol for positive, negative, occluded, ambiguous, and rejected frames.
    • Extracted the deterministic fts-pilot-v0.1 dataset: 48 pending frames from all six recordings, with source-video splits, timestamps, checksums, and six contact sheets.
    • Added an FTS ground-truth annotation workspace to the dashboard. It displays each pilot image, supports mouse-drawn boxes and No FTS/Unsure decisions, and saves YOLO labels plus an append-only decision history without modifying the original image.

    Method and rationale¶

    The append-only review log was not modified or compacted. The latest decision for each (session, candidate) pair was used because this preserves the audit trail while allowing corrections. The review queue included periodic detector locks and automatically flagged anomalies. It was used to test semantic checkpoint correctness, not to estimate frame-level precision or recall.

    Results¶

    • Review completion: 130/130 unique candidates.
    • Periodic detector locks judged correct: 37/123 (30.1%).
    • Latest verdicts across all candidates:
    • correct FTS: 38;
    • station false lock: 82;
    • wrong object: 8; and
    • tracker drift: 2.
    • Correct periodic detector locks:
    • od_red_1: 12/13;
    • od_red_2: 15/17;
    • od_blue_1: 0/29;
    • od_blue_2: 0/30;
    • od_white_1: 0/21; and
    • od_white_2: 10/13.
    • Mean detector confidence was 0.1205 for correct locks and 0.1354 for station false locks.

    Mistakes and diagnosis¶

    1. Near-complete output coverage had initially looked like stable tracking. Human review showed that CSRT often tracked a station continuously.
    2. A higher confidence threshold was considered as a simple correction, but the score distributions contradicted it because false locks were more confident on average.
    3. The append-only review contained repeated saves. The records were valid, but the interface did not clearly prevent an unchanged submission.

    Corrections¶

    • Reported the result as checkpoint correctness rather than precision or recall.
    • Rejected unchanged live transfer of the single-reference YOLOE configuration.
    • Added disabled-state feedback for unchanged review decisions.
    • Defined verified ground truth as the next prerequisite before detector tuning.

    Validation¶

    • Ruff formatting and linting passed.
    • Full test suite passed before journal automation: 30 tests. After adding and validating the journal updater and its regression coverage, the full suite passed with 34 tests.
    • Editor diagnostics reported no errors in changed report, test, JavaScript, and documentation files.
    • Browser validation confirmed that an unchanged decision is disabled and a changed note enables “Save corrected decision” without submitting a new row.
    • Review report regeneration confirmed 130/130 candidates and 37/123 correct detector locks.

    Interpretation and limitations¶

    The baseline cannot be called 30.1% precise or 30.1% accurate over all frames. The reviewed sample was a queue of detector checkpoints and flagged anomalies, not exhaustive frame-level ground truth. The evidence is sufficient to reject the current method for unchanged live use, but not to compare final detector precision and recall.

    Next step¶

    Manually classify and annotate all 48 pilot frames using the new protocol. Complete a second review of validation and test items, then add targeted frames for any missing empty-scene, strong-motion-blur, difficult-lighting, or occlusion conditions before freezing fts-ground-truth-v1.
